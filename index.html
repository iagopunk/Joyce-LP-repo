What is Rolfing?

Rolfing (SI) is a holistic form of bodywork that realigns the whole body by releasing and reorganizing the connective tissues, called fascia. It potentially resolves discomfort, reduces compensations, and alleviates pain.

Read more

A hands-on therapeutic method that addresses the whole body’s fascial network. Fascia is the tissue that surrounds your whole body, from every muscle, to your heart and lungs, encasing joints and influencing the nervous system.

Instead of focusing on symptoms, Rolfing focuses on the causes by organizing and improving the function of the whole body. It has a unique approach of working the relationships of the body, such as how the feet influence the hips or how the pelvis position affects the shoulders.

The Rolfer therapist uses different strategies, like movement and mild pressure to release the fascial network and restore balance and structural alignment in the body.

The Rolfing process enables the body to regain the natural integrity of its form, thus enhancing postural efficiency and your freedom of movement.

Who Can Benefit From Rolfing?

Rolfing can benefit everybody, regardless of age, activity level, or history — just by improving alignment. No matter your age or activity level, if you’re experiencing limiting physical discomfort, Rolfing® SI may offer meaningful relief.

For most adults, Rolfing® SI provides a lasting solution to chronic tension, misalignment, and connective tissue issues. Athletes, dancers, children, business professionals, and people from all walks of life can benefit from Rolfing® SI — improving body alignment, reducing pain, speeding up recovery from injury, and increasing freedom of movement and range of motion. It also helps improve balance, enhance performance, boost energy, reduce stress, improve circulation, and increase body awareness.

How Rolfing Works

Dr. Rolf created the Rolfing Ten-Series® — a set of ten sessions that work step by step, systematically building upon each other to improve the body’s alignment and structure. Each session prepares the body for the next, balancing and optimizing the structure (shape) and function (movement) of the entire body over the course of the series.

Sessions 1–3, called the “sleeve” sessions, strive to loosen and balance surface layers of connective tissue. Sessions 4–7 are referred to as “core” sessions and address the terrain found between the bottom of the pelvis and top of the head, including the deep tissue of the legs for its role in support. Sessions 8–10 provide an opportunity to synthesize and integrate previously established advancements into the body’s structure, in a way that encourages grace and natural coordination.

What to expect

What to Expect

Before the hands-on work on the massage table, a postural static and dynamic assessment (gait analysis) is performed to assess imbalances and misalignments of the body. Throughout the session, the client’s fascia (connective tissue) is manipulated to allow it to return to a balanced relationship with soft tissues, which allows the entire body to release compensations, reorganize, and realign.

There is a movement component that may occur within a Rolfing SI session, where clients are asked to perform slow movements while the fascia network is being manipulated. The last part of the session may be done seated on a bench, where the client’s spine is manipulated with or without spinal movements. And at the very end, a quick re-assessment is done to check the results achieved.

Rolfing Dressing Code

The Rolfing sessions are done with the client wearing a tank top and gym shorts or underwear, whichever is more comfortable for you. Women can wear a bra (or sports bra) and underpants. Men can wear shorts or jockey-style briefs.

It is advised not to wear skin oil or body lotion the day of the session — otherwise the skin will be ‘slippery,’ affecting the grip needed to access the fascia and preventing the achievement of optimum results.

Common questions

Common Questions

What is Rolfing, really?

Rolfing (Structural Integration) is a hands-on approach that works with your fascia — the connective tissue around your muscles — to help reorganize how your body holds tension, instead of treating one spot at a time.

Will this actually work for someone like me?

Rolfing tends to help most with chronic, ongoing patterns rather than acute injuries — things like neck and shoulder tension, scoliosis-related discomfort, low back and hip tightness, jaw tension, and the wrist and forearm tightness that builds up from years at a keyboard. If your pain keeps showing up in the same places, or seems to move around without ever fully resolving, that’s usually a good sign this approach is worth exploring. Joyce can help you figure out if your case is a good fit during your first conversation.

Is it painful?

The Rolfing method has been modernized over the years and become a more contemporary, non-invasive type of therapy. Sessions are done according to each client’s tolerance and particularities, such as age, level of fitness, and medications taken. Joyce works to release the fascia in layers, one layer at a time — as we say, “peeling onions.” The amount of pressure applied is also subject to client feedback: you guide the therapist according to your tolerance level. Most clients feel a sensation of relief instead of pain, and depending on the circumstances, only a light touch is needed to obtain great results. As one client put it: “the old adage of Rolfing being painful is no longer true.”
