What to expect

What a Session Actually Looks Like

Rolfing has a reputation for being intense. In practice, sessions are guided by your breath and your tolerance. Joyce works at a pace your body can integrate, communicating throughout the session and adjusting pressure as needed. Most clients describe it as firm and focused — often deeply relaxing, not painful.

You can book a single session with no commitment beyond that. For deeper, lasting change, Rolfing traditionally works through a structured series — often called the Ten Series — that moves from loosening surface tension, through the body’s deeper support structure, to integrating everything so it moves as one connected system. Joyce can also work in a shorter series of 3 or 6 sessions if that fits your goals better. A series is something she’ll recommend once you’ve felt what a session can do — never something you’re required to commit to upfront.

What this could look like for you

Small, Concrete Things You Might Get Back

Rolfing isn’t about chasing the total absence of pain — it’s about getting your body to work with you again, instead of against you. Clients usually come in hoping for small, specific things:

Driving for hours without the tension creeping in before they’ve even arrived.

None of this happens overnight, and Rolfing isn’t a guarantee. But it’s built around exactly this kind of change: not just less pain in the moment, but a body that holds itself differently over time.

Common questions

Common Questions

What is Rolfing, really?

Rolfing (Structural Integration) is a hands-on approach that works with your fascia — the connective tissue around your muscles — to help reorganize how your body holds tension, instead of treating one spot at a time.

Will this actually work for someone like me?

Rolfing tends to help most with chronic, ongoing patterns rather than acute injuries — things like neck and shoulder tension, scoliosis-related discomfort, low back and hip tightness, jaw tension, and the wrist and forearm tightness that builds up from years at a keyboard. If your pain keeps showing up in the same places, or seems to move around without ever fully resolving, that’s usually a good sign this approach is worth exploring. Joyce can help you figure out if your case is a good fit during your first conversation.

Is it painful?

Sessions are guided by your breath and your tolerance, not a fixed intensity. Most people describe it as firm and focused, not painful. As one client put it: “the old adage of Rolfing being painful is no longer true.”
